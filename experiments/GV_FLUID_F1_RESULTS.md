# GV Fluid Instability Benchmark — F1 Results

## Outcome

Preregistered result:

**NOT SUPPORTIVE**

All detectors produced identical median warning lead times at every preregistered noise level.

Median paired GV advantage:

\[
\Delta L = 0
\]

at 0%, 1%, 5%, and 10% observational noise.

## Important Post-Hoc Diagnostic

Inspection of the results revealed a benchmark-design confound.

Control amplitudes were sampled from:

\[
A\in[0.10,0.40],
\]

while test amplitudes were sampled from:

\[
A\in[1.0,2.0].
\]

Because detector thresholds were calibrated exclusively on the lower-amplitude controls, test trajectories began outside the control distribution.

The observed median warning lead times were approximately equal to the typical analytic shock times:

\[
L \approx t_*.
\]

This is consistent with alarms occurring at or near the initial observation time.

Therefore the benchmark did not cleanly measure precursor detection. It primarily measured an initial distribution difference between controls and test trajectories.

## Scientific Interpretation

The preregistered F1 claim is not supported.

The result will not be reclassified or retroactively altered.

However, because of the identified amplitude-distribution confound, F1 should not be interpreted as a strong test of whether GV can detect developing spatial instability.

A new experiment must be preregistered separately.

## Next Experiment

F1b should use control and unstable trajectories with overlapping initial observable distributions.

The primary purpose of F1b will be to prevent any detector from succeeding solely because the unstable trajectories begin at larger amplitude or larger initial diagnostic values.

No F1 thresholds, metrics, or success criteria will be retroactively modified.
